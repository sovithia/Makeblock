mBot2 Robotics Program — the six-year arc

One kit family, six years. Grades 4–6 run on the mBot2 Box 1 alone, in blocks: command it (G4) → make it sense and decide (G5) → make it autonomous and networked (G6), each available as a 1-hour or 2-hour weekly course. Grades 7–9 then add precision and hardware: the wheeled mBot2 on blocks (G7) → the tracked Rover with AI and expression (G8) → the same Rover put to work with its grip arms, in full Python (G9). Every year is a progressive 20-session ladder ending in a scored public event — the four milestone cards per grade show the ladder; the capstone card is where it lands.

Grade 7 · mBot2 · wheeled base · Block-based coding

Foundations — Think Like a Robot

25 steps · 44 hours · mBot2 · wheeled base · Block-based coding

Milestones along the way

Finish it — sensors, shield, first power-on
Command 100 cm, measure the miss with a ruler — a tolerance, in hour three
  • The robot passes all four QC points, signed by another team
  • Sensors mounted straight and plugged into the right ports
The robot keeps score
Drive it by hand and it replays the route by itself — a list, made visible
  • The counter goes up once per wall, not once per loop
  • The total is on screen and labelled
Name a behaviour
Line following, written as a block the students named themselves
  • At least two custom blocks, used more than once each
  • One of them reports its result through a variable
Blocks that take a number
One variable decides what the robot is doing — a state machine, age 12
  • A block with a number input, used with three different values
  • turn_by(90) measured against the built-in, three runs each
★ Session 20 — Rescue Run (the year's conclusion)
An autonomous mission: follow the line, avoid a surprise obstacle, stop precisely in the rescue zone, celebrate. Two scored attempts + a short team presentation.
  • Two official attempts completed and scored phase by phase
  • The configuration was frozen in writing before the first attempt
  • Every student can say what their robot did and why it failed where it did

Grade 8 · mBot2 Rover · tracks + grip arms · Block-based coding

Interaction & AI — Robots That Listen and Work

24 steps · 44 hours · mBot2 Rover · tracks + grip arms · Block-based coding

Milestones along the way

Build it — chassis, head, shells, arms
Same brain, rebuilt on tracks — and every number from last year is now wrong
  • Both tracks turn freely by hand, neither binding
  • Motor cables crossed correctly, checked by another team
Checkpoint 2 — list, loop, operator, state
Two sticks and sixteen buttons, and why a controller must be asked, not told
  • Every student demonstrated all three challenges
  • Every job diagram has an arrow out of failure
The Rover speaks and listens
Say a word and the robot obeys — speech matched against a vocabulary it holds
  • The robot speaks and shows the network state on screen
  • It repeats back what it heard, correctly, at least sometimes
When it does not understand
Voice, controller and sensors all feeding one job machine
  • An explicit no-match path that speaks and does not move
  • Two lookalike commands found and one of them changed
★ Session 20 — A Job Worth Doing (the year's conclusion)
An autonomous job the team specified themselves — navigate, pick something up with the arm, deliver it, and take an instruction from a person by voice or controller. Scored against the team's own written criteria.
  • Two official attempts scored against the team's own signed criteria
  • Configuration frozen in writing before the first attempt
  • Every team told one honest failure story and what it taught them

Grade 9 · mBot2 Rover · tracked base + twin grip arms · Full Python

Engineering & Autonomy — Robots That Work

20 2 h sessions in 5 units (40 h) — every session is a ready-made one-page teacher script (timed flow, code, pitfalls, success criteria). Skills stack unit by unit and land on a scored public event in Session 20.

Milestones along the way

Session 6 · P-Control
Proportional control defeats the wobble planted in Grade 7
  • P-follower completes laps visibly smoother than bang-bang
  • Gain table with 5+ tested values, one-change discipline
Session 7 · Measure It
Engineering arguments are graphs, not opinions
  • Both error-time plots produced from real logged data
  • Written conclusion cites numbers from the graphs
Session 11 · Pick & Place
The arms earn their keep: grip, carry through a turn, release on target
  • Rung 2 (carry & release) reached by every team
  • Rung 3 attempted with logged one-change attempts
Session 13 · Navigate + Manipulate ✱
The full pipeline in miniature: navigate, detect, pick, deliver
  • Full pipeline ran end-to-end at least once
  • Both seams named with their observed failure mode
★ Session 20 — Robots at Work (the year's conclusion)
An autonomous mission simulating a real job: follow a line under closed-loop control, detect a colour marker, pick an object with the grip arms and deliver it — pitched and scored at Demo Day.
  • Every team: two scored attempts + a complete 5-minute pitch
  • Failure stories told and applauded
  • Scoreboard archived; three-year arc closed
