បង្រៀន — គំនិតគោល

The Rover has no ultrasonic and no GPS. Everything it knows about where it is comes from counting how far its own motors have turned, and that is called dead reckoning — the same method that crossed oceans before satellites. The arithmetic is one line: a wheel of diameter D advances π·D for every full turn, so degrees convert to centimetres. But do not compute the constant from the sprocket — measure it. Command a long straight, lay a tape measure along what the robot actually did, and divide. Theory gives you a number; the floor gives you the right one, and on tracks those two are never the same because rubber slips.

Then the hard truth, and it is the one the capstone is built on: error accumulates and never cancels. Drive a 50 cm square four times round and measure the gap between where the robot started and where it finished — distance and heading, three runs, a spread rather than a number. Nothing is broken; skid steering scrubs the tracks sideways on every turn and a little of each turn is simply lost. That spread is the budget every later behaviour must live inside, and it is precisely why the mission ends by stopping on a colour marker rather than by counting centimetres. Dead reckoning gets you near. A sensor gets you there. Write that sentence on the board and leave it up until Session 20.
Python (mBlock → របៀប Python)
import mbot2, cyberpi, time DEG_PER_CM = 12.4 # MEASURED, not copied def turned(): return abs(mbot2.EM_get_angle('EM1')) def drive_cm(cm, speed=40): mbot2.EM_reset_angle('EM1') v = speed * (1 if cm > 0 else -1) while turned() < abs(cm) * DEG_PER_CM: mbot2.drive_speed(v, v) time.sleep(0.01) mbot2.EM_stop() for i in range(4): # the square again drive_cm(50) mbot2.turn(90)
ប្រយ័ត្ន
  • Function names below follow mBlock’s CyberPi Python API; minor names vary by version — the class rule is: trust the editor’s autocomplete.
  • The Rover’s motor cables are crossed, so EM1 is not necessarily the side you expect. Read the sign once, then let abs() carry it and stop worrying about which is which.
  • Every team gets a different DEG_PER_CM and that is correct. A borrowed constant is the fastest way to lose an afternoon.
  • Re-measure on the venue floor before Demo Day. Tile, carpet and a dusty hall are three different constants.
  • Low battery changes the answer too. Note the charge level next to the number or the calibration expires silently.

18′Build drive_cm() closing the loop on the encoder; verify it at 20, 50 and 100 cm against the tape.
10′The square, three times: mark the start pose in tape, measure the closing gap and the heading error each run.

① ពិនិត្យឡើងវិញ និងកែកំហុស 10′

Predict the closing gap before running the square. Most pairs guess a centimetre or two; the real answer is usually far worse, and the gap between the guess and the tape measure is the entire lesson. Then predict again for a square driven at half speed, and find out whether slower really is straighter.

④ ពង្រីក — តាមស្តង់ដារ 25′
  • ±2 cm at three distances drive_cm() checked at 20, 50 and 100 cm against a tape measure, not just at the distance it was calibrated on.
  • 3 runs, spread stated The closing error given as a range — "between 6 and 11 cm" — because one run is an anecdote.
  • heading error too The final heading measured as well as the position. Heading error is the one that ruins the next leg.
⑤ កំណត់ហេតុវិស្វកម្ម 10′

DEG_PER_CM with the floor surface, the battery level and the date beside it, then the three closing errors. Session 13 stops the robot at a marker and Session 18 watches drift accumulate over a whole mission; both of them come back to this page, and a constant recorded without its conditions is a constant that will lie in a hall with a different floor.

⑥ ការត្រួតពិនិត្យការរចនា 15′

Teams state their error budget and what it forbids. Criteria — can they name one mission design that their own number rules out? A team that has measured 9 cm of drift and still plans to place a block by dead reckoning alone has not finished reading its own data.
