Demo run-of-show — Grade 8 · A Job Worth Doing

The story in one line: Grade 7 made the robot work; Grade 8 makes it work for you. One job course, one Rover, three moments, about ten minutes. This demo sells the sentence a spreadsheet cannot — you can talk to it, and it does the job — and it ends with a manager holding the controller.

Needs the Rover add-on pack. This is where the second box earns its budget line, so let it be visible: the tracked build, the grip arms, the controller. If the audience has just watched the Grade 7 demo, name the difference out loud — same brain, second box, and a robot that now takes instructions.

The three moments

#StepWhat they seeWhy it lands
1G8 Step 17
Say a word		 Say “fetch” to the Rover and it sets off — no tablet, no phone, no cable “That is running on the robot. A student decided which words it knows.” The flagship, and unlike last year’s version it is untethered. Nothing else in the kit lands like this with adults
2G8 Step 23
The job		 It drives to the pick-up zone, grips the block, carries it across, sets it down in the delivery zone and reports “Nobody is touching it. That is a year of work doing something useful.” Managers understand a job completed instantly, and the arm makes it physical in a way driving never is
3G8 Step 11
Controller		 Hand a manager the controller and let them drive the lane; the buttons still work the arm while they steer They stop watching and start participating. The most memorable ninety seconds of the day — always end here

What to build

From the kitsClassroom
One tracked Rover — mBot2 kit + add-on pack, including the grip arms and the Bluetooth controller. Ultrasonic mounted. Charged the night before. Two taped zones about a metre apart · a foam block sized to the gripper · 3 cardboard boxes for the driving lane · masking tape · Wi-Fi the robot can actually reach

Prep week

Mon–TueBuild the Rover. If it is not built yet, this is the whole week. Two traps: the track modules mirror each other, and the motor cables are crossed on the Rover — the opposite of the wheeled build.
WedRedo turn calibration on tracks, then find the arm’s safe range by hand and put it into constants. Build the job: Step 4 arm blocks → Step 22 the full job.
ThuConnect to the venue Wi-Fi and test speech in the actual room, at the demo’s time of day. Two clean run-throughs. Freeze and save as demo-g8-job-final.
Fri AMPre-flight below. Re-test speech in the morning — the room sounds different when it is full.

What will go wrong, and what to do

Check this first, today: the Wi-Fi. Speech recognition is a cloud call — the robot sends the audio away and gets words back. No internet, no moment 1. Test it on the venue network, not your home one, and find out whether the guest network needs a browser login the robot cannot perform. → If the venue Wi-Fi cannot be trusted, bring a phone hotspot and pair the robot to it during prep week. If speech is out entirely, moment 1 becomes the controller and you open on moment 2.
The Rover build is the long pole. The tracked build is hours, not minutes, and it gates the entire demo. → Start it first, not last. If it is not standing by Tuesday, run the Grade 7 demo alone this round rather than show a half-built robot.
A room full of people sounds nothing like an empty one. Recognition drops sharply with background noise, and it will be at its worst exactly when the audience arrives. → Stand close, say one clear word, and choose command words that sound nothing alike. Rehearse with the room talking. Have the robot show what it thought it heard, so a miss is visible rather than mysterious.
Servo arms strip if forced. Visitors will grab them. → Never drive the arm past resistance, and say “you can drive it, the arms are for looking” when you hand the controller over.

Friday morning pre-flight

  • Rover fully charged, controller charged, spare batteries to hand
  • Robot on the venue Wi-Fi, with network connected? showing true on its screen
  • Speech tested this morning, in this room, with people talking
  • Zones taped, foam block in place, driving-lane boxes placed
  • Arm limits confirmed and the guard tested with a deliberately bad angle
  • The whole job run once, silently, end to end
  • Controller paired and driving — or moment 3 already swapped out
  • Printed leave-behinds: the demo tour and the Grade 8 materials page

Leave-behind

Print these and have them on the table — they answer the two questions managers ask after the robot stops moving: what does the year look like, and what do we have to buy.
· The demo tour — the step ladder for each grade
· Grade 8 materials — both boxes, tagged by source
· The course itself — 24 steps, 42 hours, every hour accounted for

Running both demos back-to-back? Grade 8 goes second. Run Grade 7 · Rescue Run first on its own mat, clear it, then bring people to this course. The handover line is the whole pitch: “That one works on its own. Now let me show you the one you can talk to.” Budget about 20 minutes for the pair.
