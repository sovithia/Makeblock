Teach it — the concept

A list does not have to hold instructions. Fill one with a reading per lap — distance, time, battery — and you have a dataset the robot collected itself. add during the run, then read it back afterwards.

And the CyberPi can draw it. bar chart, add data puts one bar up per value, so the whole run appears on the robot's screen with no tablet and no spreadsheet. Seeing your own data plotted by the machine that gathered it is a different experience from copying numbers into a table, and it is the moment measurement stops feeling like homework.

Watch out
  • Decide what to measure before writing any blocks. Data collected without a question is data nobody reads.
  • Five laps is enough to see a shape and short enough to repeat. Twenty is a lesson in boredom.
  • If the bars all look the same, that is a finding too — it means whatever they changed did not matter as much as they thought.
