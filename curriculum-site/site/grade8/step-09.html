Smooth, not stepped

Teach it — the concept

Last year the robot had three speeds and jumped between them. Today it has as many speeds as there are positions, because the speed is calculated: the encoder says how far it has travelled, subtract that from the target and you have how far is left. Multiply that by a number and drive at the answer. Far from the target, fast. Nearly there, slow. No steps, no jerk.

Two things make it real. Clamp it, or a long run produces a speed of 400 and the last centimetre produces a speed of nothing. And tune the multiplier: too small and it crawls the whole way, too big and it is back to slamming. That number is yours to find, and finding it by trying is the point.

Watch out
  • This is speed from distance remaining, not steering from line error. Grade 9 does the steering version and it is their headline lesson — do not pre-empt it here.
  • Both clamps or neither. A team that clamps only the top will eventually drive backwards.
  • Showing what is left and the speed together on screen is what makes the relationship visible. Without it this is just a formula.
  • The Rover has no distance sensor — that was the mBot2's ultrasonic, and it stays in the tray. What the Rover knows is how far its own motors have turned, which is enough to ease to a stop on a measured run.
