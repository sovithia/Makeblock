Teach it — the concept

A loop inside a loop multiplies. repeat 3 wrapped around repeat 4 runs the inside twelve times, and the question to ask before every single run is "how many times does the inner block happen?" A student who can answer that reliably has understood loops; one who cannot will guess for the rest of the year.

It is also how patterns get built without repetition. Three laps of a square, with a flash between laps, is two loops and four blocks — the alternative is thirty-six blocks and a mistake somewhere in the middle of them.

Nested loops
A loop inside a loop. The inner one finishes completely on every single pass of the outer one, which is why three of an outer loop around four of an inner gives twelve — a pattern that shows up any time work has rows and columns.
Watch out
  • Ask "how many times?" before every run, every time, until it is automatic. It is the single best comprehension check in the course.
  • A counter inside the inner loop settles arguments instantly. Insist on it rather than letting teams debate.
  • Clear floor space. Three laps of a square is a lot more travel than teams expect.
