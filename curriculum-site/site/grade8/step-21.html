Teach it — the concept

A buffer hour is available here and is deliberately not in the timetable above — add it only if the class is not level. The stated hours are what the course takes when nobody needs catching up. If the class is level, spend it on the extension: coaches make the robot ask a follow-up question when it is not sure — “Did you say fetch?” — and act on the answer.

Last gate before the mission. Stage E assembles a real job out of everything above, and the students who struggle there are always the ones who never quite understood how the inputs reach the state machine.

Coaches, hands off, buffer where needed.

Watch out
  • Challenge 1 is the test of whether the vocabulary really is data. If they have to add a block, their design is not there yet.
  • Challenge 3 is the one that shows whether AI has been understood or merely operated.
  • Last checkpoint — if anyone is still behind, plan the mission teams so they are paired with a coach rather than carried by one.

Lesson flow — minute by minute

10′Set up. Say plainly that the mission starts next.
✓A class that knows the mission starts next.
30′The three challenges: (1) add a new spoken command and map it to an existing job without changing that job's blocks. (2) show what your robot does when it mishears, and explain why it must not guess. (3) explain how a spoken command reaches the job variable — and why that is the same path a button press takes.
✓Three challenges shown: a new spoken command mapped to an existing job without changing the job itself, a demonstrated mishearing that does not guess, and the path from a spoken word to the job variable.
20′Pairing: everyone through all three coaches someone who is not.
✓Everyone through all three is coaching someone who is not.
◉ Expected result at the end of this session
Teacher check
  • Every student demonstrated all three challenges
  • Every robot has a sensible no-match behaviour
  • The class starts the mission together
