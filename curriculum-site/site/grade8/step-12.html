Teach it — the concept

Add the Bluetooth Controller extension and the sticks become numbers you can read. joystick LY is not on or off — it is how far, right now. Feed the left stick to the left track and the right stick to the right and you have tank steering, built out of two numbers and the scaling you learned in Step 9.

A number that changes continuously has to be asked for, over and over, inside a forever loop. There is no hat block that fires "when the stick is at 47". That is polling, and it is the opposite of the event you have used all year: when button A pressed lets your code sleep until the robot wakes it. Steering cannot sleep. Almost every real system does some of each, and knowing which a job needs is the skill.

The controller also drives this Rover with no program at all — that is built into the robot. What you are doing today is different and better: your mapping, your blocks, on buttons you chose.

Events vs polling
There are two ways to notice something happened. An event hat wakes the program up when it happens. Polling means asking, over and over inside a loop, whether it has happened yet. Neither is better — but you have to know which one a device gives you, because a controller with no event hats can only be polled, and a poll that runs too slowly misses things.
Watch out
  • The extension is not there by default — bottom of the block palette, + extensions, Bluetooth Controller. Without it the blocks simply do not exist and the class will hunt for them.
  • Upload the program, then press A. The controller pairs with the robot, not the computer, so once it is flashed you can put the tablet away entirely — that is the point of the lesson being uploaded.
  • Divide the stick value down or the robot is uncontrollable. The right divisor is a tuning job, not a given.
  • The mapping card is not busywork — pilots write them for the same reason. A team that cannot hand their card to a stranger has not finished.
