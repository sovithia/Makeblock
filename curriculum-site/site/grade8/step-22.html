Teach it — the concept

Design a job a real robot might do: sort by colour, deliver between two zones, fetch on command, patrol and report. It must navigate, manipulate something with the arm, and take an instruction from a person — voice or controller, your choice.

The spec has to be measurable. "Works well" is not a criterion; "delivers two blocks to the correct zone in under ninety seconds, two runs out of three" is. And write your cut line today: the feature you will drop first if time runs short. Deciding it now, while it is abstract, is much easier than deciding it in the last twenty minutes of Step 23.

Watch out
  • Ban criteria without numbers. It is the single most useful rule in this step and teams will fight it.
  • Steer the human-instruction part towards whichever of voice or controller that team has working best. This is not the day for their weakest link.
  • A team with no cut line will drop something at random under pressure. Make them choose now.

Lesson flow — minute by minute

10′Brief and rubric. Two or three example jobs pitched fast, to be stolen from.
✓Two or three example jobs heard fast, and permission to steal from them.
20′Write the problem and the success criteria. Every criterion needs a number in it.
✓A written problem and success criteria where every single criterion contains a number.
20′Task breakdown: each task names the block that will do it. Circle anything with no block behind it.
✓A task list with the block that will do each one beside it, and a circle around anything with no block behind it.
10′Sign-off: which task is riskiest, and what is your cut line? Vague answers send the spec back.
✓A named riskiest task and a stated cut line. A vague answer sends the spec back.
