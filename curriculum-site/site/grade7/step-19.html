Teach it — the concept

A block with an input does many jobs instead of one. drive_square(size) draws any square. Inputs come in three kinds — number, text and boolean — and the boolean one is the interesting surprise: a block can take a condition, so park(quietly?) parks with or without the sound depending on what you hand it.

Then the real point. turns left 90° until done is itself a function — somebody at Makeblock wrote it out of encoder blocks, exactly the way you are about to. Build your own, race it against theirs with a protractor, and you will never again think of a block as magic. Sometimes yours will be better, because you know your floor.

Function inputs
A function becomes far more useful when you can hand it a value. The value goes into a named slot — an input, or parameter — and the blocks inside use that name instead of a fixed number. One function with an input replaces a family of near-identical ones, and the caller decides the detail.
Watch out
  • The encoder angle keeps counting up. Reset it before the turn or the second turn ends instantly — the bug of the day.
  • Yours will overshoot at first because the wheels keep moving after the check. That is real engineering, and lowering the speed is the honest fix.
  • A protractor beats an opinion. Insist on measuring both turns rather than watching them.
