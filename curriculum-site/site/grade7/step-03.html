Teach it — the concept

There are two ways to run a program, and this year you normally use Upload. In Upload mode mBlock translates your blocks and sends the whole program into the robot; it then runs by itself with the computer disconnected. In Live mode the program runs on the computer and sends commands to the connected robot, so mBlock must stay open and the robot stops when the connection drops. Upload is like handing someone a written recipe and leaving the kitchen; Live is standing beside them dictating each step. Prove the difference by unplugging the cable at the end of this lesson.

A program is a list of instructions that run in order. That is a sequence, and swapping two lines changes what happens — which students should do once, on purpose, in the first ten minutes.

Nothing runs until something starts it. That is an event. On this robot there is no green flag and no keyboard: it starts itself with when CyberPi starts up, or you press button A or B on the robot. Say this out loud today, because everything else this year depends on it — the robot has no stage. If you want to see something, you put it on its screen.

Sequence · Event
Two ideas hold up everything else. A sequence is instructions in order — the robot does one, then the next, and swapping two lines changes what happens. An event is what starts a sequence running. Nothing runs on its own: something has to happen first — the robot powering on, a button going down. Every program you write this year is sequences waiting on events.
Watch out
  • Device check, before anything else. This course needs mBlock with an mBot2 device. That works on an Android tablet in Chrome at ide.mblock.cc — and nowhere else. An iPad cannot run it in any browser, and the mBlock mobile apps on both iOS and Android support only the original mBot, not the mBot2. Sort this out before the first lesson, not during it.
  • The device must be mBot2. If a team picked CyberPi they will get through today happily and hit a wall in Step 4 when there are no motion blocks — check every screen now.
  • Recording overwrites the previous one. Agree a signal for "recording now" or twelve robots record each other.
  • There is no green flag on a robot. Teams who ask where it is have understood the question — answer it with when CyberPi starts up.

newAdds text to the screen where the last print left off.
newTurns lights off. Worth its own block, because a light left on reads as the program still running.
newThe eyes on the ultrasonic sensor are a display of their own. Cheap, obvious feedback about what the robot thinks is going on.
✓A and B do two different things, you said out loud what B would do before pressing it, and you were right.
15′Make it yours: name, colour, sound, face. Show the robot to another team without touching their table.
newPlays a built-in light pattern and waits for it to finish.
✓A robot with your name, your colour, your sound and your face — shown to another team without touching their table.
5′Unplug the USB and press the button. It still works — that is Upload mode, and it is why nobody is tethered to a tablet this year.
✓USB unplugged, button pressed, robot still runs. Nobody is tied to a tablet.
