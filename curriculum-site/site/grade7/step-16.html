Fixing and searching the route

Goal of these two hours: The team can undo, patch and search a recorded route without re-driving it.

Teach it — the concept

A recorded route is data, so it can be edited. delete n of removes an item, insert … at n squeezes one in, replace item n swaps one out. Undo is just delete (length of route) of route — remove whatever is on the end, without needing to know how long the list is.

It can also be searched. route contains …? answers yes or no. To find where, walk it with count-with-i — and the moment you find it, break out. There is no point checking the other forty items once you have your answer, and that is a habit worth forming early.

Watch out
  • delete (length of) is the undo. Teams who type the number in have not understood it — ask them what happens after two undos.
  • Undo an empty route and the robot stops with a traceback on its screen — deleting item 0 is not a no-op, it is an error. Guard it with if (length of route) > 0, and let a team find that crash before you say so; it is the cheapest lesson in defensive code they will get all year.
  • Without break the loop reports the last match, not the first. Let a team hit that and work out why.
  • Insert shifts everything after it along. Draw the list on the board before and after.

Lesson flow — minute by minute

15′Record a route with one deliberate mistake in the middle. Do not re-drive it — you are going to fix the data.
✓A recorded route with one wrong move in the middle, kept on purpose. Nobody re-drives it.
25′Undo the last move with delete. Then undo three. Notice you never had to know how long the list was.
newRemoves the item at a position. Deleting item 0 is an error, not a do-nothing — guard it.
✓The last move gone, then three more gone — and you never had to know how long the list was.
25′Patch the middle: replace the wrong move, and insert the one you forgot. Replay and check.
✓The wrong move replaced, the forgotten one put back, and a replay that now matches what you meant to drive.
25′Search it: does this route contain a left turn? Where is the first one? Use break so the loop stops when it finds one.
newLeaves the loop immediately. Once you have found what you were looking for, there is no reason to check the rest.
✓The robot answers “is there a left turn in here, and where is the first one?” without walking the rest of the list.
20′Prove break is doing something: show i on screen and watch the loop stop early instead of running to the end.
✓i shown on the screen, stopping partway instead of counting to the end — proof that break really stops the loop.
10′Log the four list operations and one sentence on what each is for.
✓Four list operations in the log, one sentence each on what it is for.

Teacher check
  • Undo works repeatedly without knowing the list length
  • A middle move is patched with replace and the replay proves it
  • A search finds the first match and stops there
