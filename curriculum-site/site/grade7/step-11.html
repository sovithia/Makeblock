Teach it — the concept

Conditions can be built, not just chosen. and needs both to be true, or needs either, not flips it. And numbers can be worked on before you compare them: speed can be a sum, a threshold can be a calculation.

There is a gap in the palette worth finding rather than announcing: there is no ≥ and no ≤. Only greater than, less than, equals. So "at least 20" has to be written not (x < 20). Let a team hit the wall, then let them work it out — that is real logical reasoning, and it is the moment not stops being decoration.

Operators
An operator takes values and produces a new one — comparing two numbers, adding them, joining two pieces of text. They slot into any hole that expects a value, including inside each other, so a small set of them combines into conditions far more precise than any single block could offer.
Watch out
  • Test all four combinations of two conditions on purpose — dark+close, dark+far, light+close, light+far. Teams test one and declare victory.
  • "Dark" is a number that changes with the weather and the time of day. Re-read it at the start of every session from now on.
  • The missing ≥ is not a bug in mBlock. Ask what not(x < 20) means in words until someone says "20 or more".

Lesson flow — minute by minute

15′Cover the light sensor and watch the panel. Find the number that means "dark in this room, today" — it is not the same room it was yesterday.
✓The actual number that means “dark, in this room, today” — measured now, not remembered from yesterday.
25′Build the guard dog: only react when it is dark and something is close. Test all four combinations deliberately.
✓The robot reacts only when it is dark AND something is close. All four combinations tried on purpose.
20′The ≥ problem. Ask for "at least 20 cm" and let them look for the block. Then let them build it out of not and <.
✓“At least 20 cm” working, built out of not and < because there is no ≥ block to find.
25′Arithmetic on the way through: make the speed rise as it gets darker instead of switching between two speeds.
✓The speed rises smoothly as the room darkens, instead of flipping between two settings.
20′Then show them when light value > 50 — one hat block that does what took them three. Both are right; knowing why you would choose each is the point.
