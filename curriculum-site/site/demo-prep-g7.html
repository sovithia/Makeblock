Demo run-of-show — Grade 7 · Rescue Run

The story in one line: nine months of Grade 7 turn into forty seconds where nobody touches the robot. One mat, one wheeled mBot2, three moments, about ten minutes. This demo proves the word managers actually care about — reliable.

Box 1 only. Everything on this page runs on the base mBot2 kit. The Rover add-on box stays sealed and out of the room — a manager can watch a complete Grade 7 year without a second purchase ever appearing on the table. If they ask what the next year needs, that is the cue for the Grade 8 demo, not an interruption to this one.

The three moments

#SessionWhat they seeWhy it lands
1G7 Step 2
Calibration		 Robot drives 50 cm three times, inside ±1 cm; the team's measurement table on screen "Nine months earlier they could not do this. That is a tolerance, and they met it." Answers "is this real STEM?" before it gets asked. Cut this one first if time is short — it can be told rather than shown
2G7 Step 23
Rescue Run		 Follows the line, meets the obstacle, arcs around it, re-finds the line, stops inside the rescue zone, celebrates "Nobody is touching it. That is a whole year of skills in forty seconds." The year's payoff, and the most legible autonomy moment in the program
3G7 Step 22
Run it again, moved		 Move the obstacle to a different spot in front of them, then run the identical program again "Same code. I just moved the box. It is not following a memorised route — it is following a rule." The single cheapest proof that this is programming and not choreography. Most adults silently assume the robot is replaying a recording until this moment

Timing — about 10 minutes

0–3′Moment 1 — calibration. Short, factual, sets credibility
3–7′Moment 2 — Rescue Run. The curriculum scores two official attempts, so a first-run miss is on-message, not a failure. Say that before you press start
7–10′Moment 3 — move the obstacle, run again. End here, on the rule

What to build

From the kitClassroom
One wheeled mBot2 (Box 1). Ultrasonic and Quad RGB mounted and straight. A second built robot as hot spare if you have one. Charged the night before — the shield battery level changes the calibration numbers. Line-following map, or black tape on light card · masking tape · one cardboard box as the obstacle · matte red paper for the rescue zone (glossy tape reads badly) · ruler · stopwatch · a tablet only if you want to show the code — the runs themselves need no tablet

Prep week

MonAssemble and charge. Write and save the program: S15 three-state follower → S16 zone stop and button start → S19 obstacle detour and line re-find.
TueTape the mat in the actual demo room. Calibrate the Quad RGB on that floor, under that light. Tune the detour arc with the obstacle at both marked positions. Photograph the taped layout so you can rebuild it exactly.
Wed–ThuTwo clean run-throughs end to end, in order, including the obstacle move. Then freeze the parameters and save as demo-g7-rescue-final.
Fri AMPre-flight checklist below. One silent full run before anyone arrives.

What will go wrong, and what to do

Floor surface and light change everything. Numbers tuned on one floor will not hold on another, and direct sun or a shiny floor wrecks line following. → Calibrate on Tuesday, in the room, at the demo's time of day. Recalibrate on Friday morning. The calibration ritual is 80% of line-following reliability.
Calibration is distance-based, so battery level changes it. A robot that ran all morning will undershoot the 50 cm and may stop short of the zone. → Full charge, and do not leave the robot running while people arrive.
The moved obstacle is a live variable. An arc tuned at one position can miss the line at another, which turns your best moment into your worst. → Tune at both marked positions and demo only those two. Mark them on the mat with tape so you cannot improvise under pressure.
A curled or taped-over map gives false readings. Curled edges and glossy tape both fool the Quad RGB. → Tape the map flat at every edge; matte paper for the red zone.

Friday morning pre-flight

  • Robot fully charged, and the spare too
  • Mat taped down flat, matching Tuesday's photo
  • Quad RGB recalibrated this morning, in this room, under this light
  • Program uploaded in Upload mode and run once, silently
  • Button start confirmed — B starts, A stops, no re-flashing between runs
  • Both obstacle positions taped and both tested
  • Rescue zone patch and start line down
  • Printed leave-behinds: the demo tour and the Grade 7 materials page
