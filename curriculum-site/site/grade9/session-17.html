Teach it — the concept

Build continues (arm behaviors onto the navigation spine), but the hour’s signature event is the cross-team code review — the professional practice, scaled down. Pairs of teams swap tablets and read each other’s code against a four-line checklist: functions with clear names? · constants instead of magic numbers? · comments that say why? · no copy-pasted near-duplicates? Reviewers write two ‘strengths’ and two ‘suggestions’ — the discipline of kind precision.

Frame it before anyone reads a line: review is about the code, not the coder; every suggestion is a gift that saves a future debugging hour. The checklist is the capstone rubric’s code-quality section verbatim — so today is a free mock exam. Teams then get sprint time to act on what review found; acting on feedback is the half of the skill that classrooms usually skip.
Watch out
  • Model one review comment style first: ‘pick() is very readable’ / ‘GAIN=0.5 appears 3× — constant?’
  • A team ‘too busy to review’ is the team that most needs it — the checklist takes 12 minutes, hold firm.

① Recap & Debug 10′

Set the tone before anyone reads a line: review is about the code, not the coder, and every suggestion is a gift that saves someone a future debugging hour. Model one comment of each kind aloud — a strength and a suggestion — so that nobody's first attempt at this comes out as a personal remark.

④ Extend — to spec 25′
  • 2 strengths, 2 suggestions Written, specific, and pointed at named functions or lines rather than at the file in general.
  • one applied this hour A suggestion acted on before the session ends — not noted down for a later that never arrives.
  • all 4 checklist lines Clear function names, no repeated magic numbers, comments saying why, dead code removed.
⑤ Engineering Log 10′

The review received and the review given, plus the change made in response. The checklist is the capstone rubric's code-quality section word for word, so this hour is a free mock exam and this page is the mark sheet.

⑥ Design Review 15′

Teams report what they actually changed. Criteria — was the suggestion applied or merely acknowledged? The team that says it is too busy to review is reliably the team that most needs it, and the checklist takes twelve minutes.
